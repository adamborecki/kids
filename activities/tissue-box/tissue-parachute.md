# 🪂 Tissue Parachute Drop

Make a parachute from **one tissue** 🪂 … how slowly can it fall?

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 🤧 Tissues
- [ ] 🧵 Thread or thin string
- [ ] 🟨 Tape
- [ ] 🧸 A tiny toy or a paperclip (the jumper)
- [ ] ⏱️ Timer

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Cut 4 strings 🧵
All the same length (about 25 cm).

### 2️⃣ Tape to corners 🟨
One string to each corner of the tissue.

### 3️⃣ Attach the jumper 🧸
Tie all 4 strings to the toy or paperclip.

### 4️⃣ Drop it! 🪂
From the same height every time (top of a couch, held high). Time the fall.

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 7️⃣ Age 7
Drop with and without the parachute. Which is slower?

### 8️⃣ Age 8
**Time it** ⏱️ 3 drops each. Write the times.

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Bigger is better?** 📏 Try a 1-tissue vs. 2-tissue parachute.

### 🔟 Age 10
**Air hole** 🕳️ Cut a small hole in the middle. Does it fall straighter?

### 🚀 Age 11+
**Design contest** 🏆 Slowest fall wins. Change one thing at a time and record each test.

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 🪂 The tissue catches air. Air pushes up (that's **air resistance**).
- ⬇️ Gravity pulls down. More air caught = slower fall.
- 🕳️ A small center hole lets air escape evenly, so it wobbles less.

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- 🪜 Drop from a safe height — a grown-up can drop from stairs. No climbing on furniture!
