# 🤧 Sneeze Science

How far does a sneeze go? 🤧💦 Let's test with a spray bottle — and see why **tissues** (and elbows) matter!

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 💦 Spray bottle with water + a few drops of food coloring
- [ ] 📄 Big sheets of paper (or paper towels) on the floor
- [ ] 🤧 A tissue
- [ ] 📏 Tape measure

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Lay out the paper 📄
A long strip on the floor.

### 2️⃣ Fake sneeze 💦
Stand at one end. Spray **mist** 3 times forward, at nose height.

### 3️⃣ Measure 📏
How far did the colored drops land?

### 4️⃣ Cover the sneeze 🤧
Spray again — through a tissue held in front.

### 5️⃣ Compare 👀
Where are the drops now?

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 6️⃣ Age 6
See the drops. Practice the **elbow sneeze** 💪

### 7️⃣ Age 7
Mark the farthest drop with a sticker. Measure it.

### 8️⃣ Age 8
**Cover test** 🤧 No cover vs. tissue vs. elbow (spray into a sleeve). Which stops the most?

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Hand wash** 🧼 Rub a little paint on your hands. Wash for 5 seconds vs. 20 seconds. Which gets it all?

### 🔟 Age 10
**Poster** 📢 Make a "Cover Your Sneeze" poster with your results.

### 🚀 Age 11+
**Research** 🔍 Real sneezes can spray droplets several meters. Look up why that matters for colds and flu. Write 3 facts.

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 🤧 Sneezes blast tiny droplets out fast — farther than you'd think!
- 🦠 Germs ride on those droplets.
- 💪 Elbows and tissues catch most of them.

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- 🎨 Food coloring stains — use paper on the floor, or do it outside.
- 🧴 Use a separate spray bottle for colored water.

## 🧽 Cleanup
- [ ] 📄 Toss the paper
- [ ] 🧽 Wipe stray drops
