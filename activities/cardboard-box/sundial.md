# 🕰️ Cardboard Sundial

Tell time with **the sun!** ☀️🕰️ Just cardboard, a pencil, and a sunny day.

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 📦 A square of stiff cardboard
- [ ] ✏️ A pencil (the pointer, called a **gnomon**)
- [ ] 🖊️ Marker
- [ ] 🪨 Rocks (to hold it down)
- [ ] ⏰ A clock
- [ ] ☀️ A sunny day

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Poke the pencil in ✏️
Push it straight up through the middle.

### 2️⃣ Find a sunny spot ☀️
Flat ground that's sunny all day. Weigh it down.

### 3️⃣ Mark every hour ⏰
At 9, 10, 11… draw a line on the shadow. Write the time.

### 4️⃣ Next day 📅
Don't move it! Now tell time by the shadow.

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 8️⃣ Age 8
Mark 4 hours. Check it the next day!

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Shadow length** 📏 Measure the shadow every hour. When is it shortest?

### 🔟 Age 10
**Accuracy test** ⏰ Check your sundial against a clock a week later. Is it still right?

### 🚀 Age 11+
**Point it north** 🧭 Real sundials tilt the gnomon toward the North Star (in the northern hemisphere). Look up why — and why sundial time can differ from clock time.

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 🌍 The sun seems to move across the sky because **Earth spins**.
- 📏 Shadows are shortest around midday, when the sun is highest.
- ⏰ Sundials and clocks don't match exactly — time zones and daylight saving shift clock time.

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- ☀️ Don't look at the sun. Look at the shadow!

## 🧽 Cleanup
📦 Bring it inside if it might rain
