# 🌱 Sprouts

A real game invented by mathematicians in 1967! 🧮 Just dots, lines, and sneaky thinking. 🌱

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 🖊️ 4-color pen (one color per player)
- [ ] 📄 Paper
- [ ] 👫 2 players

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Draw 3 dots ⚫
Spread them out on the paper.

### 2️⃣ Your move ✏️
Draw a line from one dot to another (or loop back to the same dot).

### 3️⃣ Add a new dot 🌱
Put a new dot somewhere **on the line you just drew**.

### 4️⃣ The rules 📏
- Lines can't cross other lines.
- A dot can only have **3 lines** touching it. Then it's "dead."

### 5️⃣ Last move wins 🏆
If you can't make a move on your turn, you lose!

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 7️⃣ Age 7
Start with 2 dots. Learn the rules.

### 8️⃣ Age 8
Start with 3 dots. Play best of 3.

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Count moves** 🔢 How many moves did the game last? Play 3 games — what's the most?

### 🔟 Age 10
**Spot the trap** 🪤 Before each move, count how many "lives" (open line spots) are left.

### 🚀 Age 11+
**The math** 🧮 A game with **n** dots always ends in at most **3n − 1** moves. Check it: with 3 dots, max 8 moves. Can you figure out why? (Hint: each dot has 3 lives, each move uses 2 and adds 1.)

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 🧮 Sprouts was invented by mathematicians John Conway and Michael Paterson.
- 🗺️ It's about **topology** — the math of shapes and connections.
- 🔮 Good players think 2–3 moves ahead.

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- 📄 Draw big — lines get crowded fast.
- 🤔 Disputes about "did it cross?" are part of the fun. Use different colors per player to see.

## 🧽 Cleanup
🖊️ Click the pen closed!
