# 🔍 Water Drop Magnifier

One drop of water = a **tiny magnifying glass!** 🔍💧

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 💦 Spray bottle (twist to **STREAM**, or use a single drop)
- [ ] 🎞️ Clear plastic wrap or a clear lid
- [ ] 📰 Newspaper or a book with small print
- [ ] 🔢 The printable

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Cover the words 🎞️
Lay plastic wrap flat over some small print.

### 2️⃣ Make one drop 💧
Squeeze slowly so one drop lands on the plastic.

### 3️⃣ Look down 👀
The letters under the drop look BIGGER!

### 4️⃣ Try sizes 🔁
Small drop vs. big drop. Which magnifies more?

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 7️⃣ Age 7
Find the drop that makes letters biggest.

### 8️⃣ Age 8
**Tiny treasure hunt** 🔎 Look at a leaf, salt, and fabric through a drop.

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Drop size test** 📏 Make 3 drop sizes. Which makes letters biggest? Draw what you see.

### 🔟 Age 10
**Make a magnifier stand** 🏗️ Tape plastic wrap over a cup's opening. Put objects under it.

### 🚀 Age 11+
**Lens science** 🔬 Why does a rounder drop magnify more? Look up **convex lens** and explain it with a drawing.

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 💧 A drop is round on top, like a lens. It **bends** light.
- 🔍 Bent light makes things look bigger — just like a magnifying glass.
- 🔬 Early microscopes (1600s) used tiny glass beads the same way!

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- 📰 Big print is boring — use the tiniest print you can find.
- 🔬 Great intro before a real magnifier or microscope.
