# 👕 Spray Tie-Dye Shirt

Turn a plain white shirt into a **wearable rainbow!** 🌈👕

## 🧰 Gather
- [ ] 👕 White cotton shirt (pre-washed)
- [ ] 🎨 A tie-dye kit (with dye bottles)
- [ ] 💦 Spray bottles — **only** for dye, labeled DYE
- [ ] 🧤 Gloves
- [ ] 🔗 Rubber bands
- [ ] 🛍️ Plastic bag + plastic tablecloth

## 👣 Steps

### 1️⃣ Follow the kit 📦
Some kits want the shirt soaked or damp first.

### 2️⃣ Twist or scrunch 🌀
Spiral: pinch the middle, twist like a cinnamon roll, add rubber bands.

### 3️⃣ Spray the dye 💦
Different colors on each "slice." Flip and spray the back.

### 4️⃣ Bag it 🛍️
Seal in a plastic bag. Wait 6–24 hours (check the kit).

### 5️⃣ Rinse + reveal ✨
Rinse in cold water until clear. Cut bands. TA-DA!

## 🎚️ By Age
🎂 Pick the age. Every age can do the main steps — this is the **extra challenge**.

### 8️⃣ Age 8
Spiral pattern with 2 colors.

### 9️⃣ Age 9
**Choose your pattern** — spiral 🌀, stripes ➖, or crumple 💥.

### 🔟 Age 10
**Color wheel** 🎨 Put colors next to each other that mix well (blue-green-yellow). Avoid opposite colors touching (they make brown!).

### 🚀 Age 11+
**Design first** ✏️ Sketch the shirt on the printable. Plan colors. Then make it match.

## 🧠 Why It Works
- 🎨 Dye bonds to cotton fibers — it doesn't just sit on top.
- 🔗 Rubber bands block dye, making white lines.
- 🟤 Opposite colors (red + green, blue + orange) mix into brown. Neighbor colors stay bright.

## 🛟 Grown-Up Notes
- 🧤 **Grown-up job:** mixing dye powder. Gloves on. Outside only.
- 🧴 Dye bottles are **never** used for water again.
- 👕 Wash the shirt alone the first time — it bleeds.

## 🧽 Cleanup
- [ ] 🧤 Toss gloves
- [ ] 🗑️ Fold up the tablecloth (dye side in)
- [ ] 🚿 Rinse spray bottles outside
- [ ] 🧼 Scrub hands — dye can stain for a day
